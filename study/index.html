The LearnLog study, in plain terms

The source code is public, but reading it is a big ask. This page sits in between: what we record, why, and how to check that we are telling the truth.

What we are trying to find out

The short version

Last semester we watched students use AI on a single assignment. How they prompted predicted how well they understood the material that day — and predicted nothing at all about what they still knew a week later.

That gap is the whole question. Something about the way people work with these tools decides whether the understanding sticks, and nobody yet knows what it is. A single assignment is too short a window to see it, so this semester we are following AI use across an entire course, and recording what students' brains do while they work.

How it works

Four independent gates. Data only exists if all of them are open.

1
You create an account and give consent

You choose your own email and password — we never see the password. Until you tick the consent box in the extension's settings, nothing at all is recorded. You can withdraw consent from the same page and recording stops immediately.

2
You say yes to a specific chat

The first time you send a message in a new conversation on ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, a small box asks whether to log it. Say no, or ignore it, and that whole conversation is discarded — including the messages you already sent. You are not asked about it again.

3
You allow each file separately

Attach something to a logged chat and a second box asks whether to include the file itself. Decline and only its name, type and size are recorded — the bytes never leave your machine.

4
You can pause any time

One switch in the popup halts everything. While paused, nothing is captured and nothing is held — events are dropped at the source, not queued for later.

One detail worth knowing

The consent box appears after your first message, because until you have said something we do not know a new conversation exists. Those opening messages are held inside your browser while the box is on screen. If you say yes, they are released and logged with the rest. If you say no, they are destroyed without ever reaching the network.

How you worked, not just what you wrote

This is the part people do not expect, so we are stating it plainly

Understanding whether AI use helps learning means knowing more than the words exchanged. A prompt typed slowly after thinking is a different event from one pasted in ten seconds. So for a chat you have agreed to log, the extension also records timing and interaction patterns.

SignalWhat it means
Reading timeGap between the AI finishing its reply and your first keystroke.
Composition timeFirst keystroke to sending.
Edits while composingHow many times you deleted while writing a message. Not what you deleted — only how often.
Pastes into the boxHow many times you pasted text in.
Copying out of a replyThat you copied text matching a recent AI reply, and roughly how much.
AttentionWhether the tab lost focus while a reply generated; idle stretches over a minute; returning after time away.
Scrolling backThat you returned to an earlier message, and how long you stayed there.
Regenerate and editThat you asked for another answer, or edited an earlier message.

All of it is confined to the logged conversation. None of it captures keystrokes, clipboard contents, or anything on any other page.

Never recorded, under any circumstances

  • Chats you declined, or any chat while the extension is paused
  • Anything at all before you sign in and consent
  • Any website other than ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini — the extension has no permission to run anywhere else
  • Your passwords, your clipboard, or your browsing history
  • Anything outside a Chrome tab: phone apps, Copilot, Cursor, VS Code and the rest are invisible to it

The tabs permission that Chrome mentions at install is worth explaining, because it sounds worse than it is. It is used in exactly one way: to send a message to a tab that is already on one of the three AI sites. The query is filtered to those three addresses, so the extension cannot enumerate your other tabs or see their addresses.

Where the data goes, and who can see it

Logs are held briefly in your browser, then uploaded to a Supabase database. Database rules mean a signed-in participant can read their own rows and nobody else's — that restriction is enforced by the server, not by the extension, so it holds even if someone modifies their copy of the code.

Your data is anonymous. Nothing shared, published, or seen by any other researcher can be traced back to you. Dr. Saurabh Gandhi is the only person able to connect an account to a name, and that link is removed before the data goes anywhere else. Anonymised data may be shared and reused for further scientific research beyond this course; once released that way it can no longer be traced to you, and therefore can no longer be withdrawn — so if you want your data removed, ask before the end of the semester.

Your participation never affects your grades

Your prompts are never marked, never shown to anyone assessing your work, and never used for or against you. Taking part, skipping it, or quitting halfway changes nothing about your standing in this course.

At the end of the semester we do compare study data against course performance — that is how we find out whether AI use relates to what people actually learn. It runs one way only: your marks inform the research, and the research never touches your marks.

The two groups

Participants are randomly assigned to one of two groups. About six weeks in, one group starts receiving occasional feedback on their own prompting habits — specific suggestions aimed at the kind of understanding that survives the week. The other group does not.

Neither you nor we choose which group you land in; assignment is random, and that is the point. If everyone received feedback there would be nothing to compare against, and the study could not answer its own question. If you are in the group that does not receive feedback during the study, you get the same material once the study closes. Everyone gets it. The difference is timing.

Questions

Including the awkward ones

Will any of this affect my marks?

No. Your prompts are never marked and never shown to anyone assessing your work. Signing up, not signing up, or withdrawing in week nine has no effect on your standing. Course performance is used at the end of the semester as a research measure — it tells us whether AI use relates to learning. It never runs the other way.

Can my instructor read what I typed and know it was me?

Dr. Gandhi holds the only link between accounts and names, so in principle yes. That is the honest answer and you should know it before signing up. In practice the analysis is done across the cohort, not by reading individuals, and the identity link is stripped before any other researcher sees anything. If a particular conversation is one you would rather nobody saw, answer no to the box, or pause.

Does it record my other browsing?

It cannot. A Chrome extension can only run where its manifest permits, and LearnLog's manifest lists ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. Other tabs are not merely ignored — the code has no access to them at all. You can verify this in manifest.json without knowing how to program: the list of sites is right there.

Why does Chrome warn me about it?

Because for now you install it manually rather than from the Chrome Web Store, and Chrome shows that yellow banner for every manually installed extension regardless of what it does. It is a statement about how the extension was installed, not about what it contains. A Web Store version is a few weeks away and we will send it to everyone when it lands.

What happens to the messages I send before the box appears?

They wait inside your browser. Say yes and they are logged with the rest of the conversation; say no and they are destroyed without ever being uploaded. The box has to come after your first message because until then there is no conversation for it to ask about.

I forgot to pause and logged something personal. What now?

Email Dr. Gandhi and it will be deleted. You do not need to say what it was or why. This happens, it is expected, and it is not a problem.

Can I see what you have on me?

Yes — open the extension's settings and scroll to "My data". Everything held about you is listed there and you can download the lot as a spreadsheet or data file. Deletion goes through Dr. Gandhi rather than a button, deliberately: the database has no delete permission for participants, which is also what stops anyone deleting someone else's rows.

Does it work on my phone, or with Copilot?

No. It only sees ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini in a Chrome tab on a computer. Phone apps, Copilot, Cursor and editor plugins are invisible to it. That is a genuine limitation of the study and we would rather state it than pretend otherwise.

Can I use it on more than one computer?

Yes. Install it and sign in with the same account on each — everything lands under one participant identity regardless of which machine you were using.

What if I lose internet?

Nothing is lost. Events queue on your own machine and upload when you reconnect.

I do not think I am very good at using AI. Is that a problem?

The opposite. A study made only of confident power users would tell us almost nothing. What we need is the honest range, which means people who are unsure, people who barely use it, and people who use it constantly. There is no standard to meet and nothing you can do wrong.

Should I use AI differently now that I am being watched?

Please do not. Behaving unusually for the study makes the research worse, not better — we are trying to observe how people actually work. If a chat is one you would rather not have logged, say no to it. Otherwise carry on exactly as you were.

Do I have to do the brain study?

No. It is optional, separate, and has its own consent process. Saying you are interested on the sign-up form commits you to nothing, and declining later affects nothing.

Can I quit partway through?

Yes, at any point, without giving a reason. Withdraw consent in the settings page and recording stops immediately. Tell Dr. Gandhi if you also want the data already collected removed.

What happens to all this at the end?

Anonymised data is analysed, written up, and made available for other researchers to reuse — that is normal practice and it is how findings get checked by people other than us. You will get a personalised summary of your own AI use, and the findings will be shared with the class.
